# Report 5: loading on the way and stopovers, 4 arms × 11 seeds

Runs from 2026-11-11: `uv run apeks-run experiments/*_sweep.toml --snapshot-every 10 --logs logs/r5`. The setup is the same as report 4: seeds 0–10, 6000 ticks, the same events and demand, and `claude-haiku-5-5` for the LLM arms. All 44 runs finished, with 0 LLM failures and 0 skipped calls.

Report 4's loading rule had two flaws:

- A ship filled spare space with the most common waiting destination, wherever that port lay, so packages could ride the wrong way.
- A convoy sailed past ports on its route without stopping, even when it carried packages for them.

Report 5 changes the fixed cargo heuristic in every arm:

- **Loading on the way.** A ship loads for its target port first. That is the most common destination aboard, or the most common waiting destination if it is empty. It then loads for other ports in order of detour, the extra charted time of here → port → target over here → target. It skips any port whose detour exceeds `DETOUR_FRACTION` = 0.25 of the direct time. Space it cannot fill on the way stays empty.
- **Stopovers.** A convoy that reaches a port where a member has packages to deliver makes that port its destination. It berths, unloads and regroups there.

## Findings

1. **The fix helps every arm a little, and LLM routing reliably.**
   - LLM routing gains 10 deliveries and loses 82 ticks of latency, both in 11/11 seeds.
   - The other arms move the same way, but not significantly.
   - Every arm sails with emptier holds: the load factor falls by 0.07–0.12, because space the ship can't fill on the way stays empty. Every arm also sails 37–49% less distance empty.
   - Energy per delivery falls in both LLM-routing arms (p=0.001).
2. **Stopovers barely matter on this map.** There are 1–3 per run. Nearly all of the gain comes from loading only on the way.
3. **The routing gap is unchanged.** LLM routing still delivers about 85% more than classical, at 39% of its latency and half its energy per delivery.
4. **LLM formation now shortens latency** (−159 ticks, 10/11 seeds, significant after Holm). Its near-miss excess (+95, 1/11 seeds better) is now significant after Holm too, which confirms the safety finding of reports 3 and 4.
5. **The combined arm trails LLM routing alone** by 5 deliveries (p=0.020, uncorrected), with more near misses. The formation module costs a little in the combined arm.

## Limits

- The same as report 4: one map, one demand level, 11 seeds, one model.
- `DETOUR_FRACTION` was not varied. A larger value fills holds at the cost of longer trips; a smaller one empties them further.
- The r4–r5 comparison for the LLM arms mixes the heuristic change with run-to-run LLM variation. The classical arm is deterministic per seed, so its differences come from the heuristic alone.

## Next steps

- Sweep `DETOUR_FRACTION` (0, 0.25, 0.5, 1) to find the trade-off between load factor and latency.
- Sweep `demand` to locate each arm's saturation point.
- Trace the LLM-formation near misses, which are now confirmed.
