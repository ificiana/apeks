## Information, radio and LLM use

- **Radio:** 99–102k messages per run, 5.0% of receptions lost, no overflow.
- **Policy spread:** 330 versions were decided across the LLM arms, and 308 reached the whole fleet. The median was 41 ticks.
- **LLM use per module per run:** 7.4–7.5 calls, 22–24k input and 5.9–7.9k output tokens, about $0.006–0.007.

## Findings

1. **Classical saturates; LLM routing does not.** At 1.5× demand, classical delivers 173 of 455 packages (+29 over report 5) and its backlog doubles to 111. Both LLM-routing arms deliver 397–405 (+130–143) and keep the backlog near 20.
2. **The routing gap widens.** LLM routing now delivers 2.3× as much as classical, at 37–38% of its latency and 40% of its energy per delivery, all in 11/11 seeds.
3. **Holds fill up.** The load factor rises to 0.74 for classical and 0.62 for the LLM-routing arms. The empty-distance excess of LLM routing shrinks to about 2–2.6k and is no longer significant after Holm.
4. **LLM formation no longer differs from classical.** Its latency gain and its near-miss excess from report 5 are both gone at this demand.
5. **The combined arm now edges LLM routing alone** by 8.5 deliveries (p=0.013, uncorrected), the reverse of report 5. Its near-miss count is pulled up by one outlier run.

## Limits

- One map, one model, 11 seeds, and now two demand levels.
- Across reports, the LLM arms mix the demand change with run-to-run LLM variation.
- The reversal between the combined arm and LLM routing alone is small and uncorrected. It needs more seeds before it counts.
