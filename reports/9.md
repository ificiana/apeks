# Report 9: LLM routing against classical as the map grows, 5–250 ports

Runs from 2026-11-11 on the 80-core server (`ai104`), at commit `ddbb625` with the r9 seed and port lists in `experiments/classical_sweep.toml` and `experiments/llm_routing_sweep.toml`:

`uv run apeks-run experiments/classical_sweep.toml experiments/llm_routing_sweep.toml --snapshot-every 10 --logs logs/r9 -j 80`

The sweep took 589 s of wall time. Two arms (classical: ACO routing and Boids formation; llm_routing: Claude Haiku 5.5 routing and Boids formation), ports 5/10/50/100/250, adjacency 3, seeds 0–10, 6000 ticks, 110 runs. LLM budget is 20 policy calls per run and effort is the CLI default. Each seed gives the same map, demand and hazards to both arms, so every comparison is paired by seed.

Maps are generated (`mapgen.py`): each port has 4 berths and 3 ships, and every 5 ports add 1 storm, 1 lane closure and 2 currents. Demand per port is fixed, so generated packages grow with ports (452 at 5 ports, 22 565 at 250).

- **Simulation cost is roughly linear in ships.** Without the LLM, classical goes from 2.2 to 60.6 ms per tick for 50× the ports (15 → 750 ships), about ships^0.85. Movement (Boids and physics) is the largest phase from 50 ports on, then radio and ACO (`convoys`).
- **Claude time is capped by the budget.** The `llm` phase is 7.5–10 s per call: 7.5 calls (56 s) at 5 ports, 14 at 10, and the full 20 calls (about 199 s) from 50 ports up. It is 40–85% of an llm_routing run.
- **The ACO phase grows faster than the map**, 2.2 → 72 s from 5 to 250 ports, as report 8 found: fading sums over lane reports that are never trimmed. With LLM routing, convoys follow the policy and the phase stays at half that.
- **Map generation** takes under 10 s at 250 ports.
- **The whole sweep fits in 10 minutes** because all 110 runs ran at once on 80 cores; the slowest run (250 ports, llm_routing) took 520 s.

- **Up to 10 ports**, LLM routing calls Claude 7–14 times per run without using up the budget. Nearly every new policy reaches every ship, in a median of about 40 ticks.

## Findings

1. **LLM routing beats classical at every size.** It delivers 3.4× (5 ports), 4.1× (10), 10.6× (50), 13.5× (100) and 15× (250) as many packages, with every seed agreeing at every size. Backlog, arrivals, load factor, energy per delivery and near misses favour LLM routing at every size from 10 ports on.
2. **Classical stops delivering on larger maps.** It delivers 57–131 packages at every size from 5 to 250 ports, while demand grows 50×, so its delivered share falls from 21% to 0.3%. Its load factor drops to 0.34. One likely cause, not tested here, is that ACO's signal spreads thin over hundreds of lanes.
3. **LLM routing peaks at 100 ports and falls back at 250.** Deliveries go 931 → 1 086 → 863, and the delivered share falls 21% → 12% → 4%. This coincides with the budget being spent early and policies never reaching the whole fleet.
4. **LLM routing pays in congestion on large maps.** It concentrates traffic, with 2.1× (50 ports), 2.7× (100) and 3.4× (250) the congestion ticks of classical. Every seed agrees from 50 ports on. Classical's lower congestion probably reflects that its ships complete far fewer legs (arrivals are 10–14× lower).
5. **Latency does not separate the arms** except at 10 ports (−917 ticks). Delivered packages take 1 250–2 570 ticks either way, and on big maps only the short trips finish within 6000 ticks.
6. **Cost is small.** An llm_routing run costs at most US$0.29 in Claude tokens, and r9 cost about US$5.7 for 55 runs.

## Limits

- **11 seeds per cell.** Holm-corrected Wilcoxon p cannot go below 0.049, so the t-tests carry the strength of the evidence.
- **One budget (20 calls) and one model.** From 50 ports the budget, not the model, sets how often policy changes; the decline at 250 ports may be a budget effect.
- **Radio overflow** from 50 ports means policy spread is limited by the radio model as much as by routing. Overflow appears in both arms but only matters for llm_routing, which depends on policies spreading.
- **6000 ticks is short for the larger maps.** Backlog grows all run for both arms at 50+ ports, so these are transient results, not steady state.
- **Both arms use Boids formation**, so this isolates routing only.
- **The `llm` phase** is wall time waiting on the Claude CLI, which varies with service load; per-tick cost of llm_routing is not reproducible to the millisecond.

## Next steps

- Scale the LLM budget with the map (for example calls per 10 ports) and rerun 100 and 250 ports to test whether the 250-port decline is budget-limited.
- Give radio queues room to match fleet size, or relay policies via ports, and check how many ships each policy reaches.
- Trim ACO's report lists (incremental faded sums) so classical stays cheap at 250+ ports, and check whether classical's collapse is the ACO signal or the cost model.
- Run longer (20 000 ticks) at 50+ ports to see whether either arm reaches a steady backlog.
- Add the combined and llm_formation arms at 50 ports to see whether the LLM formation policy reduces LLM routing's congestion.
