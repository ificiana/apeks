## Inference

### Method

Every arm ran the same 11 seeds, and a seed fixes the scenario's randomness (fleet, radio losses, ACO), so the arms are compared **seed by seed**: for each seed, take the LLM arm's value minus classical's. With n=11 pairs:

- **Wilcoxon signed-rank test** on the differences, the main test; it assumes no normality. Its smallest possible p-value at n=11 is 0.001.
- Paired t-test as a cross-check.
- 95% bootstrap confidence interval of the mean difference (10 000 resamples).
- Effect size d_z = mean difference / sd of differences. Above 0.8 is large.
- **Holm correction** across the 18 comparisons against classical (6 metrics × 3 arms). A result marked ✓ is significant at 0.05 after correction.

The LLM's replies vary between calls, so a seed does not fix an LLM arm's run exactly. That variation is part of what the tests measure.

### Is the combined arm just the sum of its parts?

The four arms form a 2×2 design (routing: ACO or LLM × formation: Boids or LLM). The interaction is (llm − llm_routing) − (llm_formation − classical), computed per seed. For deliveries it is −0.8, 95% CI [−4.4, 3.7], Wilcoxon p=0.33. That is no detectable interaction: **the two LLM modules' effects add up.** LLM routing adds about +17 deliveries, LLM formation costs about −8, and the combined arm lands near 18.6 + 17 − 8 ≈ 27.7 (observed: 26.9).

The combined arm is worse than LLM routing alone on deliveries (−8.8, CI [−12.6, −5.1], p=0.003) and trip time (+142.5, p=0.007), and uses less total energy (−30 683, p=0.001). Per delivery, the two are not distinguishable (+452, CI [−3, 959], p=0.10), and energy per delivery shows no interaction either (−677, CI [−2 431, 913], p=0.90).

## Findings

1. **LLM routing is a large, reliable win.** It beats classical on deliveries in all 11 seeds, by 17 on average (+92%), and on trip time in all 11 (−41%), with very large effect sizes (d_z 2.7–2.9). Its total energy is not distinguishable from classical's, so it spends about half as much per delivery (−4 483, −48%, lower in all 11 seeds). It is also the most consistent arm: trip-time sd is 51 against 135 for classical.

2. **LLM formation is reliably worse at delivering.** It delivers fewer than classical in every seed (−8, −43%). It uses 36% less total energy, but only because its ships move less: per delivery it is no more efficient than classical (+1 129, p=0.32). Seed 0's near tie in report 1 (17 vs 18) was its best result relative to classical. Its congestion reduction from report 1 does not hold: the difference is not significant (p=0.83).

3. **The combined arm is additive.** There is no interaction between the modules; the combined arm's deficit against LLM routing is the formation module's cost, not a clash between the two policies.

4. **Safety improves in every LLM arm, but only the combined arm passes correction.** All three LLM arms cut near misses by 38–49% on average. Only the combined arm (−69.5, all 11 seeds, p=0.001) survives the Holm correction; the other two (p=0.042) are suggestive.

5. **Congestion is too noisy to call.** Both routing arms point to less congestion (median 151 and 345 against 563), but the per-seed spread is large and neither result is significant.

6. **Avoiding the strait from the start is not where the routing win comes from.** Runs whose first policy used the strait did no worse (p=0.21 and 0.30). Report 1's worry about prompt-derived foresight does not explain the result.

7. **Keeping the strait closed after it reopens may cost deliveries.** In `llm_routing`, runs that reopened it delivered more (median 38 vs 31.5, p=0.044). This is one post-hoc test, not replicated in the combined arm (p=0.52), so it is a lead to test, not a finding.

## Limits

- 11 seeds per arm. The Wilcoxon floor of p=0.001 is reached by every 11/11 result; stronger evidence needs more seeds, not a different test.
- One scenario and one model. The results say nothing yet about other maps, event schedules or models.
- An LLM run is not reproducible from its seed: `llm_routing` seed 0 delivered 38 in report 1 and 39 here.

## Next steps
