# Report 8: classical arm on generated maps, 5–40 ports

Runs from 2026-11-11 on the 8-core laptop: `uv run apeks-run experiments/classical_scale.toml --snapshot-every 10 --logs logs/r8`. The sweep took 183 s of wall time. One seed (24008), the classical arm only (ACO and Boids), 6000 ticks, ports 5/10/20/40 × adjacency 2/3/4, 12 runs. The aim is to measure how the cost of a tick grows with the size of the map before LLM arms run at 20+ ports.

The maps are generated (`mapgen.py`). The area grows as √(ports/5) from 1000 × 700, and each port has 4 berths and 3 ships. Every 5 ports add 1 storm, 1 lane closure and 2 currents. Demand per port is unchanged (1.5×), so generated packages grow with ports (439 → 3609).

### Fixed before the sweep

A first probe showed the `ports` phase at 61 ms per tick at 20 ports (k=4), against 0.4 ms at 5 ports. Each port picked destinations by running one Dijkstra per port, and every Dijkstra recomputed every lane's drift-integrated transit estimate. That made the cost O(ports × lanes × samples × currents) per regroup, about ports³.

Lane estimates are now computed once and shared (`Network.lane_times`, `World.lane_costs`). They are recomputed only when the drift changes, that is when the set of active storms or the ocean changes. Dijkstra walks an index of incoming lanes instead of scanning every lane. Summaries are identical before and after the change (checked on p10_k3, p10_k4 and p20_k4). The ports phase is now 0.02–1.2 ms, and at 20 ports a tick costs 5–10 ms instead of 120–215.

### How each phase scales (15 → 120 ships, 13 → 84 nodes)

- **radio** grows about 16× for 8× the ships, close to ships² over pairs in range: 0.3 → 4.8 ms.
- **safety** (near-miss checks) grows about 45×, pairwise among ships: 0.04 → 1.85 ms.
- **move** grows roughly linearly: 0.2 → 2.4 ms.
- **ports** is now small and grows with ports × lanes: 0.02 → 1.2 ms.
- **convoys** varies by 30× between runs of the same size (0.4–25.6 ms) and dominates at 10 and 40 ports. Profiling at 10 ports puts the cost in `routing.pheromone`, at about 16M `exp` calls per 500 ticks. Each ACO decision sums an exponentially fading term over every lane report the convoy has heard, and those lists only grow during a run. The cost therefore depends on how much the convoys have talked and how often they re-decide, not just on map size.

### Expected time beyond 40 ports

Excluding the convoys phase, a tick costs about 0.6 ms at 5 ports, 4.5 ms at 20 and 11 ms at 40, growing slightly faster than ships^1.3. Radio and safety, which go as ships², take over. A rough extrapolation gives 35–45 ms per tick at 80 ports, so 4–5 min per 6000-tick run before ACO's report sums. With the ACO term as observed, add 5–25 ms at 40 ports and probably more at 80.

A 50-seed sweep of 4 arms at 40 ports would take about 200 runs × 2–3 min, roughly 10 min on the 80-core server. The LLM arms add their own call latency on top.

## Findings

1. **The simulation is cheap once the duplicate lane estimates are gone.** All 12 runs fit in 3 minutes on a laptop. Excluding ACO, a tick costs about 11 ms at 40 ports.
2. **Radio and safety are the scaling limit.** Both are pairwise in ships and grow about as ships². Beyond 40 ports they will dominate unless a spatial index (grid buckets) limits pairs to ships within range.
3. **ACO's cost grows with the run, not just the map.** Fading report sums over a list that is never trimmed make the convoys phase noisy and potentially unbounded. An incremental faded sum per lane (rebased exponential decay) would make it O(1) per option. It changes the floating-point results in the last bits, so it is not applied here.
4. **The classical arm nearly stops working on generated maps.** It delivers 6–11% of packages at 5 ports, against 39% (178 of 456) on the classic map in report 7, and 1–10% at larger sizes. Backlog grows linearly all run. This is one seed, so it cannot be split into map difficulty (coastal detours, longer legs) and engine weakness, but the gap is large.
5. **Adjacency has no consistent effect on one seed.** More lanes lower congestion at 40 ports (165k → 91k) and raise deliveries at 20 ports (142 → 184), but 10 ports shows no trend. A seed sweep is needed.

## Limits

- One seed per cell, so outcome differences between cells are anecdotal; only the timing trends are robust.
- Node counts grow unevenly (33 at 10 ports, 42 at 20) because bridging waypoints depend on the coast.
- Timings are from one 8-core laptop with 8 parallel processes; the server will differ.

## Next steps

- Make the ACO report sum incremental and add a spatial index for radio and safety, then reprobe 40 and 80 ports.
- Explain the classical collapse on generated maps: trace one 5-port run's legs (planned vs actual time, idle ships, empty legs).
- Run all four arms at 5 and 20 ports over a seed sweep.
